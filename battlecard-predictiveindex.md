## TL;DR for the 2-minute pre-call read

PI is 70 years old, tops G2's pre-employment grids (~4.7/~720 reviews), owns the "Talent Optimization" category narrative, and lands fast through a huge certified-partner reseller channel at $5K–$40K price points squarely in TMA's band. Its entire scientific load rests on **one 6-minute, 2-question self-report adjective checklist** (4 factors, 17 Reference Profiles) plus a 12-minute cognitive test — no motivation measurement, no task-based cognition, no role layer, and a weak bolted-on performance/engagement module (Charma, 2023) that customers report struggling to launch. **Phase-0 correction: PI is no longer "steady" — CEO Mike Zani was replaced March 26, 2026 by Lance Neuhauser, an AI/adtech outsider with an explicit AI-native rebuild mandate**, atop employee-reported "almost annual" layoffs and strategy churn. Customers don't hate PI — they *outgrow* it; sell the outgrowing. Never frontal-assault the BA managers love: flank via Experience (their weakest module), displace on science in regulated/high-stakes hiring, and win retention conversations with the line PI structurally cannot answer: **"PI tells you how they'll behave in week one; TMA tells you whether they'll still be there in year two."**

## 2. Positioning + narrative

- **Category ownership:** "Talent Optimization" is PI's invented category — State of Talent Optimization reports, certifications, courses. A genuine US mid-market narrative moat. Their story: one platform, one language (Reference Profiles), hire-to-inspire.
- **The instrument:** PI Behavioral Assessment = two ~86-adjective free-choice checklists ("how others expect you to act" / "how you really are"), untimed ~6 min → 4 factors (Dominance, Extraversion, Patience, Formality), 17 Reference Profiles. PI Cognitive = 12 min/50 questions. Job Targets = stakeholder-consensus ranges on those scores. **Precision note for reps: it's free-choice, NOT forced-choice ipsative — don't say "ipsative" or they'll rebut the detail. Say: self-report, single method, six minutes.**
- **Science posture:** EFPA-certified BA (real external credential — respect it); 400+ client validation studies claimed, r = .20–.40 criterion validity, mostly PI-authored not peer-reviewed; one global ~10K norm (consistency strength, local/industry-norming weakness; refresh recency unpublished); **their own compliance pages insist the BA be "only one data point"** — a standing concession that the field misuses it.
- **Modules:** PI Hire / Inspire / Design / Perform (Charma acquisition, Dec 2023, rebranded) / Diagnose (de-emphasized). Talent Optimization Essentials bundle (Feb 2025). Paylocity ATS integration (2025); 20+ HRIS/ATS integrations.
- **Criteria they install:** speed, simplicity, manager adoption, price, 70-year brand, category language. **Criteria they avoid:** method breadth, motivation measurement, subgroup adverse-impact tables, local validation, role architecture, engagement depth.

## 4. Feature gaps (ranked for a 500–20K buyer in TMA verticals)

1. **One instrument carries the whole scientific load.**
   (a) Evidence: product line inspection; their own "one data point" compliance hedging; IO-adjacent critiques of checklist granularity/defensibility; customer quote: managers "overgeneralize or misunderstand patterns without proper training."
   (b) Confidence: high on structure; the misuse pattern is medium-high (their own guidance concedes it).
   (c) Exploitation: in regulated/high-stakes hiring (clinicians, CU leadership, public sector), ask for the role-specific validation study, subgroup adverse-impact data, and Job Target derivation documentation. Public materials are guidance-level; TMA+BrainsFirst run role-specific validation.

2. **No motivation measurement — the retention blind spot.**
   (a) Evidence: 4 behavioral factors + cognitive is the entire measurement surface.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: turnover-pain buyers. "Your PI data didn't change, but your people keep leaving — because behavior style isn't motivation. Talent = Want x Can; PI only gestures at the Can."

3. **Experience layer is a struggling bolt-on.**
   (a) Evidence: PI Perform launch struggles in reviews; Diagnose de-emphasized; Charma acquired 2023 and rebranded.
   (b) Confidence: medium-high.
   (c) Exploitation: entry wedge #1 when PI is incumbent — land DecisionWise engagement/360 alongside the entrenched BA; expand into Person/Fit at renewal.

4. **No role layer at all.** STYR rides in free via pay-transparency/architecture conversations PI never touches. (High.)

5. **2026 instability window.**
   (a) Evidence: outsider CEO Mar 2026 with rebuild mandate; annual-layoff employee reports; strategy-churn reviews.
   (b) Confidence: documented events; forward-risk framing.
   (c) Exploitation: 3-year-deal buyers deserve the question: "what does the platform, packaging, and price look like after the AI relaunch — and will your partner still be certified on it?"

6. **Partner-quality variance.** Where the buyer's PI experience came through a weak reseller, sell direct consortium implementation quality. (Medium.)

## 5. Pricing signals

- **Model:** employee-count-based annual subscription, unlimited assessments — great optics for high-volume screening, **expensive for orgs hiring in pockets** (flat fee regardless of usage).
- **Benchmarks:** entry ~$5K; common $8K–18K at 50–200 employees; mid-market ACV est. $10K–40K. Levers: multi-year 15–30%, volume 10–20%, **Q4 urgency discounts** — time competitive bids to their fiscal Q4.
- **How to position:** PI will usually be cheaper on sticker — don't chase. Two frames: (1) **cost-per-decision-quality** — a 6-minute checklist pricing a clinical-leadership hire is the expensive option (bad hire ≈ 3x salary — approved ROI frame); (2) **usage math** — flat platform fee ÷ actual hires/year often exceeds per-candidate specialist assessment for pocket-hiring orgs. In displacement bids: their renewal + their Q4 + their 2026 repackaging uncertainty = maximum leverage moment.

- **Category-language lock-in** — orgs fluent in Reference Profiles resist re-learning; the "Talent Optimization" narrative is sticky.

- **Run-alongside assessment:** PI incumbents are sticky — never frontal-assault the BA. The close: keep PI for volume screening if they love it — the stack owns the decisions that matter.

## 9. Landmines (discovery-question form — never name PI)

1. Ask: "For the roles where a bad hire really hurts — what's the role-specific validation evidence and adverse-impact analysis behind your current assessment?" → Guidance-level public docs (§4.1).
2. Ask: "Does your assessment measure what motivates someone to stay, or how they behave? Which of your turnover problems is which?" → No motivation layer (§4.2).
3. Ask: "How did your team set the job target for that role — measured against performance data, or a stakeholder consensus meeting?" → Job Target derivation (§3).
4. Ask: "How's adoption of the performance/engagement module going — and was it built by the same company or acquired?" → PI Perform (§4.3).
5. Ask: "Your assessment vendor just changed CEOs with an AI-rebuild mandate — what's in writing about platform, packaging, and pricing through your renewal?" → 2026 window (§4.5).
6. Ask: "Who implemented your assessment program — the vendor or a reseller — and what happens if that partner relationship changes?" → Channel variance (§4.6).
7. Ask: "What does your flat platform fee work out to per actual hire made last year?" → Usage math (§5).

## 10. Objection-handling angles

**When PI is incumbent or shortlisted:**

1. *"PI is simpler and our managers love it."* → "Keep it for volume screening — genuinely. The question is which decisions deserve six minutes of self-description and which deserve measured capacity and motivation. Your clinical leads, your branch managers, your succession slate — those are 45-minute decisions, not 6-minute ones."
2. *"70 years of science, 400+ validation studies."* → "Respect the heritage — and read their own compliance pages: the assessment should be 'only one data point.' The studies are self-published client studies on one self-report instrument at r = .20–.40. Ask what a defensible selection process for your regulated roles requires: method breadth, role-specific validation, documented adverse impact. That's the stack's home field."
3. *"They're much cheaper."* → "On sticker, usually. Run the flat fee against your actual hires, and price one mis-hire at three times salary. Then ask what the platform costs after the AI relaunch — their new CEO was hired to rebuild it."
4. *"Talent Optimization is the proven playbook."* → "It's one vendor's name for stretching one instrument across the lifecycle. The parts of the lifecycle it stretches thinnest — engagement, development, role design, motivation — are exactly where their reviews and their module adoption struggle. Buy the layer, not the label."
5. *"Our PI partner is basically part of our team."* → "That relationship has value — keep the human. But ask: is the advice you're getting the best measurement for your roles, or the product your partner is certified to resell? We'll happily run a head-to-head on one critical role and let the data answer."

**FUD they'll plant — pre-armed:**

| Their line | Response |
|---|---|
| "The consortium is four vendors — we're one platform" | One platform = one 6-minute instrument wearing four module names, per their own product line. The stack enters one layer with one contract; each layer is the category specialist |
| "Games aren't science — checklists are proven" | NeurOlympics measures task performance — what people DO, not what they SAY about themselves — with 1,500+ datapoints per candidate and validity evidence. Ask both vendors for the technical manual and subgroup data; we'll table ours first |
| "We're AI-native now" | Their AI era started with a March 2026 CEO hire; the instrument underneath is unchanged since before your managers were born. AI on 4 factors is a chatbot reading a horoscope; AI on four measured layers is decision support |
| "Boutique consortium risk" | Their own employees report annual layoffs and strategy pivots, and the founder-era CEO just exited. Stability questions are welcome — both directions |

## 11. MEDDPICC angles

- **Metrics:** quality-of-hire and diversity outcomes from validated selection (TOPdesk 3→18 hires with better diversity — approved anchor), retention deltas from motivation-matched placement (TMA), redeployment economics (Atos EUR 200-300K — approved), role-specific validation coverage (% of critical roles with documented validation — a metric PI can't table publicly).
- **Decision criteria to install:** (1) method breadth per decision-stakes tier; (2) role-specific validation + subgroup adverse-impact documentation on the table; (3) motivation measurement for retention-critical roles; (4) engagement/360 depth (not module checkbox); (5) vendor roadmap stability in writing through the AI relaunch; (6) direct-vs-reseller delivery accountability.
- **Pain they leave unsolved:** why people leave (motivation), what roles should be (architecture), how leaders develop (360 craft), and any decision where "fast" isn't the same as "right."
- **Paper process / procurement:** their friction — sales-led opaque pricing, flat-fee-vs-usage mismatch, reseller-in-the-middle accountability, 2026 repackaging unknowns; your leverage — Q4 timing, per-role pilot offers ("one critical role, head-to-head, data decides"), multi-year price protection against their repricing risk.
- **Champion note:** PI's champion is the line manager who loves Reference Profiles and the local partner. Don't fight the language — bridge it (profile-mapping collateral), and build your champion in TA leadership, HR strategy, or the exec sponsor who owns turnover and succession — the people accountable for outcomes a 6-minute checklist can't carry.
