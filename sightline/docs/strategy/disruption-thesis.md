# Disruption Thesis: The Incumbents' Late Fees

## The Blockbuster mapping, in numbers

Blockbuster earned roughly $800M/yr from late fees at its peak, revenue that punished its own customers, and it could not give that revenue up to fight Netflix because the fees funded the stores. Residential security has two late fees, and they are bigger.

**Late fee (a): door-knock customer acquisition.**

- ADT, Q2 2026 filings: ~$1,800 net subscriber acquisition cost ($345M net SAC / 190K gross adds), 2.3 year payback per subscriber.
- Vivint, 2019 Form 425: ~$2,150 gross creation cost per subscriber, ~4,000 commission-only door-to-door reps, ~$300M/yr in door-to-door commissions. Vivint also paid a $20M FTC settlement over its sales practices, and was acquired by NRG for $2.8B at 6.3x EBITDA (2023), a multiple that says the market prices this machine as a slow annuity, not a growth engine.

**Late fee (b): human monitoring at retail markup.**

- Retail monitoring runs $30 to $80/mo. Wholesale monitoring available today to anyone: COPS Monitoring's SecureNet bundle at $5.99/account/mo, plus Rapid Response and Becklar/AvantGuard as alternatives. The wholesale cost of the service is $2 to $6/mo (est. range around the $5.99 anchor). The spread, roughly $25 to $75/mo per home, is the second late fee.
- The spread is defended by two structures: multi-year contracts, and debt. ADT carries ~$7.4B net debt. Debt service demands the RMR stream stay fat.

## Why they cannot self-cannibalize

1. **The sales machine funds growth.** Vivint's ~4,000 commission-only reps and ~$300M/yr commission line are not a cost to trim, they are the distribution. Cut the commission, the reps walk, gross adds collapse. ADT's 13.1% gross attrition means it must replace roughly one in eight subscribers every year just to stand still, at ~$1,800 each. The machine cannot stop knocking.
2. **$7.4B of net debt demands the RMR.** Repricing monitoring from $50 to $10 would vaporize the collateral. A leveraged incumbent cannot volunteer a 70% RMR haircut. This is the same trap that kept Blockbuster charging late fees while Netflix mailed DVDs.
3. **RMR portfolios trade at ~26x to 50x monthly RMR (Barnes).** Every internal incentive, from dealer program buybacks to exec comp, is indexed to keeping the multiple's numerator high. Self-cannibalization is a fireable offense at an incumbent.

## The price umbrella is already proven

SimpliSafe Active Guard sells agent-assisted monitoring at $49.99/mo overnight and $79.99/mo 24/7 (Oct 2024). Deep Sentinel charges $60 to $100/mo per camera and has raised ~$38M. Demand for proactive, verified monitoring at premium prices is proven. ## Why now

- **AVS-01 exists.** TMA's alarm scoring standard was ANSI-accredited in 2023 and IACP-ratified in Oct 2024. For the first time there is an industry-standard language in which a machine-generated, evidence-backed alarm outranks a human-relayed guess. Guardian scores AVS-01 level 3 on the flagship scenario in the demo today.
- **Verified response is spreading.** Seattle ignored 96% of 13K false alarms in 2023; 94 to 98% of dispatches are false industry-wide. Police departments are done responding to unverified signals. That breaks the incumbents' core product and makes verified, scored events the only alarms that will matter.
- **Agent cost curves.** Guardian's marginal compute is $0.42/home/mo modeled, $0.09 measured in the demo at simulated volumes. The cost of watching a home around the clock has fallen roughly three orders of magnitude below the retail price of doing it with people.
- **The permanent lighting boom built our installed base.** Trimlight 120K+ homes (300+ dealers, PE-backed 2025), JellyFish 50K+, plus Gemstone, Oelo, and EverLights: 170K+ homes already have a powered roofline rail that SightLine nodes retrofit into. The incumbents' door knockers cannot see this base. We can, and the dealers who installed it want year-round revenue.

The thesis in one sentence: the incumbents are structurally required to keep charging $1,800 to acquire and $30 to $80 to monitor, and SightLine acquires for $285 and monitors for $4.17.
