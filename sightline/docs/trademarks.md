# Trademark Status and Naming Alternatives

Not legal advice. Working notes for a clearance effort with trademark counsel.

## Status of "SightLine"

"SightLine" is a working name. The mark space is crowded: "sightline" is a common English word used heavily across optics, surveillance, AV, and construction. LIKELY conflicts exist in the classes we care about, especially Class 9 (cameras, electronics) and Class 45 (security services). Assume the name does not survive clearance and treat everything below as the pipeline for its replacement.

## Ten alternate names

1. Eaveline. Literal and ownable: the product lives on the eaves, the name says exactly where the protection sits.
2. Perimetra. Perimeter coverage as a coined, registrable word; reads premium and international.
3. RoofSentry. Plain descriptive power: a sentry on the roof, instantly parses for homeowners and dealers.
4. Cornice. Single real word from roofline architecture, short, elegant, and unexpected in security.
5. Fascia Guard. Names the exact mounting surface (fascia board), signals trade credibility with installers.
6. Halovant. "Halo" (ring of light around the home) plus vigilant; coined, distinctive, strong Class 9 odds.
7. Luxguard. Light (lux) as the guard: captures the deterrence-by-light mechanism in one word.
8. Ridgewatch. Watch kept from the ridge of the house; sturdy, outdoorsy, easy to say and spell.
9. Everave. "Ever" (always on, permanent install) fused with "eave"; coined and short with clean domain odds.
10. Skirtline. The lit "skirt" of the roof as a defensive line; distinctive in security, descriptive of the form factor.

## USPTO knockout search procedure

Purpose: kill weak candidates cheaply before paying counsel for full clearance.

1. USPTO search (the TESS successor at tmsearch.uspto.gov): search each candidate as a word mark. Search exact, then with wildcards (e.g. "eavelin*"), then phonetic and spelling variants (Eaveline, Eveline, Eavelyne; Perimetra, Perimetera, Parimetra). Sound-alike conflicts count.
2. Restrict and then widen by class: check Class 9 (electronic and camera hardware), Class 45 (security and monitoring services), Class 42 (SaaS, the cloud/portal side). Then run unrestricted, because a famous mark in any class can still block.
3. For each hit, open TSDR: check live/dead status, goods and services description, filing basis, and owner. A dead mark is a caution flag, not a green light (common-law rights can survive).
4. Judge likelihood of confusion, not identity: similar sound, appearance, or meaning on related goods is the standard. "RoofSentinel" blocks "RoofSentry."
5. Check state trademark registrations in launch states (state SOS databases), since federal search misses them.
6. Check common-law use: web search each name plus "security," "camera," "lighting"; check domains (.com first), and handles on the major socials. A squatted .com is a cost issue; an active competitor using the name is a conflict.
7. Score candidates: drop any with a live, similar mark in classes 9, 45, or 42 on related goods. Rank survivors.
8. Send the top 2 or 3 survivors to trademark counsel for full clearance (comprehensive search, opinion letter) before filing an intent-to-use application in classes 9, 42, and 45.

## Notes

- File intent-to-use early once counsel clears a name; the filing date is the priority date.
- Register the matching domain and socials the same day the shortlist is chosen, before any public use.
- This document is a search plan, not a clearance opinion. Final clearance is counsel's call.
