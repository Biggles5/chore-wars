## TL;DR for the 2-minute pre-call read

Korn Ferry is the one competitor that can claim all four layers under one brand: KF4D/Talent Q assessments (Person/Fit), the Hay job-evaluation method + KF Pay data (Role), KF Listen engagement (Experience), KF Coach/360 development — all wrapped in the "Talent Suite" platform launched Nov 2025. The numbers tell the real story: the Digital segment where all of that lives was **flat at ~$363.5M in FY26 (~12.5% of a $2.91B firm) and declined 2.6% YoY in Q4** while services grew 7%; only ~44% of even Digital's new business is subscription. KF Listen is delivered **"in partnership with Qualtrics and Forsta"** — the softest wall in the fortress. Consulting rates run $470–500/hr, 42% of backlog is engagements over $1M, and management publicly celebrates moving "up-brand" — a 2,000-employee credit union is not who this machine serves. The $1.1B AMS acquisition (announced Jun 2026, closing ~Oct 2026) doubles down on RPO services and dilutes the software story further. **Primary wedge: the post-engagement vacuum — consultants roll off, the binder stays, nothing operationalizes. The stack is the system that runs every day after KF leaves.** Concede board-level transformations with exec search attached; win everywhere the buyer needs a living system, not a project.

## 2. Positioning + narrative

- **One-liner:** the integrated talent firm — "50 years of IP," billions of data points, ~30M professional profiles, one brand across search, consulting, and software. [Documented, cached]
- **Talent Suite (Nov 2025):** consolidates Architect, Assess, Pay, Recruit, Sell, Listen, Coach into one subscription platform. Real launch, real CapEx ($85M FY26) — but discussed on earnings calls as *future* monetization, not current growth; the segment it lives in shrank in Q4. Analyst framing: the platform + IP is being used to justify **$500/hr consulting rates** as much as to sell software. [Documented + medium-confidence analyst framing, cached]
- **Criteria they install:** integration ("one throat to choke"), data scale (32,000-org pay dataset, 7M exec profiles, 2.5M+ assessments), brand/board access, global delivery, the Hay method as "the global standard."
- **Criteria they avoid:** Digital segment growth numbers, subscription mix, whose software runs KF Listen, per-employee pricing for a 2,000-person org, what happens after the engagement ends, G2 review counts (~35 firm-wide).
- **Strategic direction — read it plainly:** management celebrates moving "up-brand" (average fees +10%, million-dollar engagements, a stated pivot to a "$300B" enterprise talent-solutions TAM), and the AMS deal buys RPO scale, not software. Post-close, Digital falls toward ~10% of the combined firm. The four-layer *software* story is shrinking as a share of what Korn Ferry is. [Documented facts; the framing is inference marked as such]

## 4. Feature gaps (ranked for a 500–20K US buyer)

1. **The post-engagement vacuum.**
   (a) Evidence: business model sells time ($470–500/hr); what persists is the artifact (Hay grades, assessment reports, survey deck), not a living system; subscription is only 44% of even Digital's new business; Glassdoor pattern of "quarterly layoffs when the company did not meet financial goals" means the delivering consultants may not exist at renewal.
   (b) Confidence: high on economics; medium-high on the churn pattern (anonymous reviews, heavily repeated theme + publicly announced FY23 reductions).
   (c) Exploitation: "Keep the KF diagnosis — we're the system that runs it every day." Target accounts 6–18 months after a KF org-design or assessment engagement.

2. **Experience layer is rented.**
   (a) Evidence: KF's own Listen page + Qualtrics press release document the partnership; KF contributes survey IP, norms, consultants — not the platform.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: any buyer comparing listening platforms directly discovers KF isn't one. Easiest layer to win head-to-head; DecisionWise owns its platform, benchmarks, and debrief practice.

3. **Hay-graded orgs need a modern refresh.**
   (a) Evidence: enormous 1990s–2000s installed base in healthcare, government, higher ed; re-grading requires certified evaluators at consulting rates; published critiques (slow, lock-in, gender-bias literature, scarcity-blind).
   (b) Confidence: high on installed base; medium on conversion ease.
   (c) Exploitation: STYR modernizes the architecture without the certification priesthood — and pay-transparency laws give Total Rewards the budget trigger.

4. **Point-in-time assessment vs continuous talent insight.**
   (a) Evidence: KF4D fires at hire/succession moments, consultant-debriefed; no KF product path to always-on person insight at mid-market price.
   (b) Confidence: medium-high.
   (c) Exploitation: TMA's motivation-first frame plus BrainsFirst's funnel-grade measurement serve the daily talent decisions between KF's big moments.

5. **Mid-market structural abandonment.**
   (a) Evidence: management's own "up-brand" language; average fees +10%; 42% of backlog >$1M; a 2K-employee credit union gets a junior team, an off-the-shelf survey, or a referral.
   (b) Confidence: high on the strategy facts; the mid-market inference is strong but marked as inference.
   (c) Exploitation: quote their rate card against per-employee-per-year pricing. In the 500–20K band, KF competes with nobody because it isn't really there.

6. **AMS integration window (now → 2027).**
   (a) Evidence: $1.1B deal, ~$581M revolver draw, disclosed integration/retention risks, cross-border services integration of 10,000+ people.
   (b) Confidence: high on facts; attention-drain is inference.
   (c) Exploitation: "Will your org-design, assessment, and engagement roadmap get C-suite attention during a $1.1B RPO integration?" Time-box outreach into KF accounts accordingly.

## 8. Where they win (honest) + run-alongside assessment

**They win:**
- Board-mandated C-suite transformation with exec search attached (CEO succession + org redesign) — the search halo and board relationships are decisive. Don't fight it.
- Global enterprise (20K+, multi-country) needing one global comp dataset and one global vendor, with the budget.
- Public-sector/regulated buyers contractually or culturally wedded to Hay methodology.
- Enterprise RPO at scale post-AMS — out of consortium scope entirely.
**Run-alongside assessment:** the natural sequence is **after** KF, not beside them. KF does the board-level diagnosis; the stack operationalizes it — "keep the strategy deck, install the system that executes it daily." Where KF holds a listening or assessment contract, DecisionWise/TMA enter through the layers KF rents or under-serves, at a price point KF structurally won't match.

## 11. MEDDPICC angles

- **Metrics:** 3-year total advisory spend per employee vs subscription cost; % of last engagement's recommendations operationalized at 90/180 days; re-grade cost per role; engagement-survey action rates between cycles. Bad hire ≈ 3x salary and senior exit at 50–200% of salary anchor the operational stakes KF's point-in-time model leaves open.
- **Economic buyer:** KF's relationships live at board/CEO level; the stack's buyers are the CHRO's lieutenants (Total Rewards, TA, L&D, OD) who own the daily systems — sell where the operating budget lives, below KF's altitude.
- **Decision criteria (reshape):** insert "runs without consultants," "per-employee pricing," "platform ownership" (who owns the listening platform and data), and "continuous vs point-in-time" — four criteria the consulting model fails by design.
- **Decision process:** avoid RFPs KF frames as transformation programs; drive product-pilot processes (one layer, one quarter, measurable) where a services firm can't respond in kind.
- **Identify pain:** binder-on-the-shelf syndrome post-engagement; grade structures nobody can maintain; survey results with no action model; assessment reports that expire at the succession event.
- **Champion:** the HR operator who inherited the KF deliverable and has to make it real with no budget left; the CFO staring at the services line.
- **Competition:** expect brand halo and board-level air cover. Don't compete at that altitude — make the evaluation about what runs on Tuesday morning, priced per employee.
