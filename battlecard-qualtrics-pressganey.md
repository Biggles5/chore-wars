## TL;DR for the 2-minute pre-call read

On paper: patient + employee + customer experience in one company, "the world's largest AI dataset for human experience," Press Ganey's Magnet-locked nursing benchmarks (~500K RNs), 41,000 provider organizations, Qualtrics's Forrester-Leader EX suite. In documented reality: **the CEO who signed the deal stepped down two weeks after announcing it; JPMorgan's 11-bank syndicate couldn't sell the $5.3B debt package (paper losses >$500M — "the largest hung deal in leveraged finance this year") explicitly over AI-disruption risk to survey vendors; the new CEO (a finance operator, in seat since February) purged five senior execs in April; and the combined entity owns THREE overlapping EX platforms — two of which were rival Forrester Wave Leaders in the same quarter — plus InMoment, which Forrester expects to be sunset within ~2 years.** Press Ganey's own CEO says integration takes 2–3 years. That's the window, in their words. No role, person, or fit layer exists anywhere in the combined portfolio. In healthcare: never fight Magnet/HCAHPS — run alongside, sell the three whitespace layers plus 360/leadership craft, and ask one question: "which of their three employee-survey platforms will you be on in 2028, and who decides?" Outside healthcare: the merger *reduces* their dedicated presence (Forsta/InMoment carried non-healthcare, and that's the sunset layer) — mid-market is nobody's priority inside a debt-financed integration.

## 4. Feature gaps (ranked)

1. **Nobody knows which platform survives — including them.**
   (a) Evidence: three overlapping EX platforms + InMoment; Forrester's sunset prediction and RFP advice; "whether they integrate remains to be seen"; PG CEO's own 2–3 year horizon.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: the single best question in this card — "Which of their three employee-survey platforms will you be on in 2028, who decides, and what does the migration cost you?" No good answer exists yet.

2. **Debt-service pressure meets a documented escalator habit.**
   (a) Evidence: hung $5.3B deal, >$500M paper losses, loan at 86¢; pre-existing 5%+ standard uplifts and a 55% repricing case on record.
   (b) Confidence: high on events; renewal-pressure framing is inference — present as "ask how the debt gets serviced if not through your renewal."
   (c) Exploitation: procurement armor — escalator caps, auto-renew removal, multi-year protection. Being the vendor who taught them this buys trust.

3. **Three of four layers don't exist — at $19B of deployed capital.**
   (a) Evidence: no role/person/fit product anywhere across Qualtrics, PG, Forsta, InMoment.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: "the biggest experience company in the world still can't tell you whether the role is broken, the person is misplaced, or the hire was wrong." The consortium's whole architecture is the answer.

4. **360/leadership development is software, not craft** — documented training/config/cost complaints; no coaching bench. Entry wedge #1, different buying center. (Confidence: medium-high.)

5. **Mid-market abandonment risk** — enterprise DNA, reorged customer ops, integration priorities; documented mid-size price walk-aways pre-merger. TMA's band is nobody's priority account. (Confidence: medium-high.)

6. **Support + complexity themes (flagged 5–6 sources each):** steep learning curve/admin burden; opaque pricing/add-on creep; renewal escalation; tiered-but-queued support; PE-driven instability at both companies. All documented in §6–7.
