## TL;DR for the 2-minute pre-call read

Perceptyx is the enterprise employee-listening pure-play — Forrester Wave Leader (Q2 2025), ~1/3 of the Fortune 100, and the healthcare fortress (3.85M-worker benchmark across 500+ systems, ANCC Magnet partner since 2014, Leapfrog-approved safety instrument). It is also: five acquisitions renamed three times in 18 months, a C-suite completely replaced in 2025 (new CEO importing Alida alumni), ex-employees describing "layoffs every time the company misses a number," ~25% AE quota attainment, and a platform whose own help docs limit the flagship Narrative Analysis Agent to *closed* point-in-time events. **They are enterprise-only in practice (82% of reviewers >1,000 employees; six-figure ACVs; no 500–5K motion) — TMA's lower band is open field.** Their May 2025 "Activate for All" overlay (runs on top of Qualtrics/Medallia/Glint) validates the run-alongside architecture — use their own move as permission. In healthcare: never attack the Magnet survey; flank with nurse-manager 360s (their own blog admits a "nursing leadership gap"), clinical-ladder role architecture, and hiring science for the turnover their platform measures but can't fix.

## 4. Feature gaps (ranked for a 500–20K US buyer)

1. **No 500–5K motion.**
   (a) Evidence: "large enterprise" in their own PR (even Activate for All); 82% enterprise reviewer base; 6sense distribution; custom six-figure contracts; full-service staffing model tuned for 5K+.
   (b) Confidence: high (<1K), medium (1–5K).
   (c) Exploitation: in TMA's band, the question isn't features — it's "who actually staffs your account, at what minimum, with what attention?" Mid-size hospitals, credit unions, colleges, and regional manufacturers get enterprise pricing and junior coverage, or no callback.

2. **Three of four layers don't exist.**
   (a) Evidence: no role/pay-equity product or content anywhere; no selection science; Lyceum's "skill verification" is learning-comprehension for existing staff.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: their own full-loop story ("listen→act") stops at behavior nudges. "When listening says pay feels unfair, who fixes the pay architecture? When it says a unit's leader is failing, who develops them with a validated instrument? When turnover is the finding, who fixes the intake?" Three questions, three uncontested layers.

3. **360 is an automation input, not a development intervention.**
   (a) Evidence: Grow = standardized platform model feeding AI coach/nudges; acquired tech re-skinned twice; no custom competency modeling or certified debrief network in public materials.
   (b) Confidence: medium.
   (c) Exploitation: entry wedge #1 (§8). Different buying center (L&D/OD/CNO) than the survey owner.

4. **AI claims ahead of documented reality.**
   (a) Evidence: Narrative Analysis Agent limited to closed point-in-time events (their own help docs) vs "real-time intelligence" marketing; Theme Frequency "largely inaccurate regardless of survey type" (reviewer); no independent validation of the two newer agents; Humu's standalone struggles.
   (b) Confidence: medium-high on the documented limitation; the accuracy quote is a single-source review — use as a probe, not an assertion.
   (c) Exploitation: bake-off scope questions — "which agents work on live vs closed data? what validated outcome evidence exists for nudge-driven change?"

5. **Organizational churn.**
   (a) Evidence: entire C-suite replaced in 2025; co-founder out after 22 years; ex-employee reviews "layoffs every time the company misses a number," "will I be next?"; RepVue ~25% AE quota attainment; 3 platform renamings in 18 months; Lyceum integrated in 10 weeks (rebrand speed, not integration depth).
   (b) Confidence: high on facts; service-impact is forward-risk framing.
   (c) Exploitation: continuity questions for enterprise buyers — "who owned your account 18 months ago, who owns it now, and which platform name will your admins be trained on next year?"

6. **Vertical blanks: credit unions, higher ed, K-12, state/local gov.**
   (a) Evidence: zero CU evidence (Atlantic Union is a bank); no higher-ed or K-12 case studies; federal-only gov footprint.
   (b) Confidence: medium-high (absence).
   (c) Exploitation: four of TMA's five verticals are effectively uncontested by them. Healthcare is the only collision — and only at enterprise scale.

## 9. Landmines (discovery-question form — never name Perceptyx)

1. Ask: "At your size, who actually staffs your account — named consultants or a pooled team — and what's the contract minimum?" → Enterprise-only economics (§4.1).
2. Ask: "When your listening data says pay feels unfair or a leader is struggling, which system fixes the pay architecture or develops the leader — and whose product is that?" → Three missing layers (§4.2).
3. Ask: "For leadership 360s: custom competency model against your framework with certified human debriefs, or a standardized platform model feeding an AI coach?" → Grow's depth (§4.3).
4. Ask: "Which of the AI agents work on live data versus closed survey events — can we see that in their documentation?" → Their own help docs (§4.4).
5. Ask: "How many times has the platform been renamed or re-architected in the last two years, and what did each migration ask of admins?" → 3 renames/18 months (§4.5).
6. Ask: "What validated outcome data exists for AI-nudge-driven behavior change?" → Humu history + no published outcomes (§3).
7. Ask (healthcare): "Between survey administrations, who builds your Magnet leadership-development evidence — the platform, or people?" → The between-surveys gap (§8.2).

## 10. Objection-handling angles

**When Perceptyx is incumbent or shortlisted:**

1. *"They're the Forrester Leader and the healthcare standard."* → "In enterprise listening — agreed, and keep the survey. The Wave has no criterion for job architecture, validated selection, or leadership instruments because the category doesn't cover them. The problems your listening data surfaces live in those layers; that's where the stack starts, one layer at a time."
2. *"Their AI agents act on the data automatically."* → "Shipped and documented — and their docs also scope the analysis agent to closed survey events, and the nudges are ungrounded in any person or role measurement because those layers don't exist there. Ask what validated behavior-change outcomes they'll show; the nudge company they bought struggled with exactly that as a standalone."
3. *"One platform, one throat to choke."* → "It's five acquisitions renamed three times in eighteen months, with a C-suite that fully turned over last year. The consortium enters one layer with one contract and one consultant; nothing gets ripped out."
4. *"They run on top of anything — they'll cover whatever we add."* → "That overlay proves the architecture we're proposing: best-of-breed layers over an incumbent. It's also priced and staffed for large enterprises — ask the minimum."
5. *"We're a hospital — they own healthcare."* → "For listening, at scale, yes. Three questions their contract can't answer: who develops your nurse managers with a validated instrument and a human debrief; who fixes the clinical-ladder pay structure your RNs cite in exit data; who improves the quality of hires feeding your turnover number. Their own research admits the nursing leadership gap."
6. *"We're too big for a consortium."* → "Each layer is delivered by its category specialist with decades in the discipline, coordinated through DecisionWise. And ask their references at your size how many consultants actually touch their account."

## 11. MEDDPICC angles

- **Metrics:** measured leadership-development outcomes from validated 360s (their nudges have no published outcome data), participation depth (DecisionWise 91% average participation — approved anchor, attach internal citation), redeployment/selection economics (Atos EUR 200-300K; TOPdesk 3→18 hires — approved anchors), time-to-value at 500–5K scale (their enterprise onboarding vs layer-scoped entry).
- **Decision criteria to install:** (1) full potential-to-performance coverage; (2) validated instruments + adverse-impact evidence for anything touching decisions; (3) consultant hours per account in the contract; (4) platform stability (renames/migrations trailing 24 months); (5) references at the buyer's size and vertical; (6) AI capabilities scoped in documentation, not press releases.
- **Pain they leave unsolved:** everything between and beneath the surveys — leader development with instruments, role/pay architecture, hiring quality, and any org under ~5K getting real attention.
- **Paper process / procurement:** their friction — six-figure custom contracts, bundled services opacity, account-team churn risk, quarter-end desperation discounting (RepVue signal — time your negotiations); your leverage — layer-scoped entry contracts, named-consultant commitments, stability story.
- **Champion note:** the survey owner (HR analytics/CHRO staff) is their champion — don't make them defend their purchase. Build yours in L&D/OD, the CNO's office, Total Rewards, and TA — four buying centers their platform never calls on. One-layer lead, always.
