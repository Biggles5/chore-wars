Culture Amp is the mid-market engagement platform standard (6,500+ companies, ~1,540 G2 reviews at 4.5, "1B+ survey responses" benchmark) — and a wounded leader: three layoff rounds in three years (~90/60/70), a founder replaced by a turnaround CFO (Jan 2026), CRO out after ~18 months (May 2026), Blackbird valuation cut 23.5%, growth decelerated 32%→19%→10.8%, FY25 loss A$37M. Their whole war chest is aimed at Lattice and Qualtrics; **they have zero prepared counter-messaging against a four-layer consortium pitch** (DecisionWise already runs a "6 Alternatives to Culture Amp" page at them — unanswered). They measure one layer and stop: no job architecture, no pay equity, no assessments, no hiring science, no validity/adverse-impact evidence anywhere in their positioning. Four complaint themes clear the 3+ source bar: chatbot-gated support, reporting rigidity, admin burden, pricing opacity. Enter through their weakest module (360s) or the consulting gap, embed, and take the engagement survey at renewal during the instability window. Respect what's real: AI Coach is a genuine platform bet (senior AI infrastructure hires), the benchmark is genuinely huge, and end-user UX reviews are positive.

## 4. Feature gaps (ranked for a 500–20K US buyer in TMA verticals)

1. **Three of four layers don't exist.**
   (a) Evidence: product line is Engage/Perform/Develop/People Analytics/AI Coach — no role, person, or fit product anywhere; rival-confirmed (Leapsome).
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: never argue engagement features. Reframe the problem as the full potential-to-performance chain: "your engagement scores tell you people are frustrated; they can't tell you if the role architecture is broken, the person is misplaced, or the hire was wrong. Which of those can your current platform measure?"

2. **360 is a module, not an instrument.**
   (a) Evidence: review complaints (clunky, rigid reviewer admin, open-text-only Pulse format, no advanced competency evaluation); their own support docs confirm rigidity.
   (b) Confidence: medium-high.
   (c) Exploitation: the ranked #1 entry wedge — sell DecisionWise's specialist 360 program (14 competencies/13 derailers, certified debriefs) alongside the incumbent engagement survey. Threatens nothing at renewal; installs the consultative relationship that wins the renewal later.

3. **No validity or adverse-impact evidence — "People Science" is data science on surveys.**
   (a) Evidence: no psychometric validity documentation, no adverse-impact analyses, no selection science anywhere in positioning; their published methodology covers survey construction only.
   (b) Confidence: high (documented absence).
   (c) Exploitation: ask for their validity documentation in the eval. The consortium answers with published psychometrics (DecisionWise instrument, TMA assessments, BrainsFirst validated games). Their own "model agnostic" line seals it for buyers who want a model.

4. **Zero proof in TMA's five verticals.**
   (a) Evidence: no named healthcare system, credit union, university, government, or manufacturer logo found; generic review-site copy only; no FedRAMP/StateRAMP; SMS/deskless reach shipped only in 2025.
   (b) Confidence: high on absence of named logos (their engagement benchmarks for healthcare/higher-ed/gov DO exist — don't overclaim).
   (c) Exploitation: "how many named references in your vertical, at your size, will each vendor provide?" Credit unions are the cleanest kill: no CU benchmark, no CU logo, while the consortium leads with vertical norms.

5. **Support and service capacity — documented complaints, now compounding with cuts.**
   (a) Evidence: 4 flagged themes with 3+ sources each (chatbot-gated support with brutal Capterra quotes; reporting rigidity; admin burden; pricing opacity); Trustpilot 3.1 unfiltered; then three layoff rounds, middle management removed, EDI team cut, employee reviews: "instability is insane."
   (b) Confidence: high on themes; **frame instability as forward risk, not present failure** — G2 still praises support.
   (c) Exploitation: "who will your CSM be in 12 months, and what's the escalation path when the chatbot can't solve it?" Get support SLAs in writing from both vendors.

6. **Renewal economics.**
   (a) Evidence: 3–7% annual escalators standard; modular add-on stacking (scientist access, premium CSM, QBRs +10–20%); attacker pages cite renewal repricing; "$$$$$" G2 tier; paying-for-unused-modules theme.
   (b) Confidence: high.
   (c) Exploitation: arm procurement with the escalator-cap and unbundling levers (15–30% off is documented with competitive pressure) — being useful to procurement builds the champion. Then position consortium pricing as scoped to the layer actually bought.

## 10. Objection-handling angles

**When Culture Amp is incumbent or shortlisted — what your champion will hear:**

1. *"They're the standard — G2 leader, a billion benchmark responses."* → Reframe: "For engagement surveys, agreed. The question is what happens after the score arrives. Their billion datapoints are all one layer; the stack measures experience, role, person, and fit, and enters one layer at a time. Keep their survey if you love it; start where they can't help."
2. *"Four vendors sounds like an integration nightmare."* → Reframe: "You buy one layer, one contract, one consultant. Expansion is optional and earned. And note the incumbent's own structure: people-scientist access, premium CSM, and QBRs are all separately priced add-ons. There is also nothing on their side to integrate to for role, person, or fit; those modules don't exist."
3. *"They have AI now — free AI Coach."* → Reframe: "Real product, real investment. It coaches from sentiment data because that's the only data they hold. Ask what it knows about your leaders' competencies, your job architecture, or a candidate's cognitive fit; the answer is nothing. AI on one layer of data is a very confident summary of a survey."
4. *"They're cheaper than a consortium."* → Reframe: "Compare scoped to scoped: the entry engagement is one layer and typically new budget, not a platform swap. Then check the incumbent's renewal: 3 to 7 percent annual escalators and add-on stacking are documented; procurement can cap both, and we'll help them do it either way."
5. *"Switching costs — we'd lose our benchmark trend."* → Reframe: "Nothing switches on day one; run-alongside is the model. When a change does make sense, DecisionWise brings 50M+ datapoints of normative depth, so trend continuity is a managed transition, not a cliff."
6. *"They're a big stable company."* → Reframe (gently, facts only): "Three layoff rounds in three years, a founder replaced by a turnaround CFO in January, the CRO out in May, and a 23.5% valuation writedown by their own investor. They may well turn it around; while they do, ask who owns your roadmap requests and who your CSM will be. DecisionWise has operated continuously for 30 years and now has consortium backing."
