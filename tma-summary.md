## 1. Five takeaways

**1. Nobody competes with the stack — everybody competes with one layer of it.**
Across all eight cards, not one competitor fields a credible product in all four layers. Korn Ferry is the only claimed four-layer rival, and its own filings undercut the claim: Digital is ~12.5% of revenue, flat in FY26 and down 2.6% in Q4, its listening layer runs on rented Qualtrics/Forsta, and the $1.1B AMS deal pushes the firm further into services. Everyone else is a one-layer vendor with marketing adjacency to a second. The one-layer-lead sales motion is not just TMA's preference — it's the shape of the market.

**2. The Experience layer is in visible turmoil; the window is open now through 2028.**
Qualtrics+Press Ganey ($6.75B deal, closed May 2026 through a halted debt package, CEO churn, and a five-exec April reorg) must consolidate three overlapping survey platforms — Forrester is telling InMoment clients to start RFPs now. Culture Amp is the wounded leader; Perceptyx re-platformed and renamed itself three times in 18 months. Every Experience-layer incumbency is negotiable at renewal between now and 2028. DecisionWise's stability, service model, and 360 craft are the contrast story — sell continuity into chaos.

**3. The Role layer is structurally uncontested.**
Zero of eight competitors builds job architecture. Syndio — the category's pay-equity leader — admits in its own blog that client architectures are "not good enough" for equal-value comparison and routes structure work to Mercer. Korn Ferry's Hay method is the incumbent installed base, license-gated and dated. SHL sold its gov consulting arm. With pay-transparency laws and the EU directive's right-to-information forcing employers to *explain* pay, STYR enters through a door nobody else has a product for.

**4. Acquired science is the pattern — and the weakness.**
Phenom stapled Be Applied + Plum in ten weeks; Perceptyx runs five bolt-ons renamed three times; Qualtrics+PG is a $6.75B roll-up; Korn Ferry's "one platform" is its third rebrand of acquired IP. The universal counter: ask for the combined technical manual, the unified data model, the cross-instrument norms. TMA and BrainsFirst's integrated, validated instruments (NeurOlympics: ~45 minutes, 1,500+ datapoints; TMA's Want×Can frame) are the thing acquisition sprints cannot manufacture.

**5. The 500–5K band is nobody's home turf but ours.**
PI tops out on depth, SHL and Phenom price at enterprise minimums ($25–50K+/yr; $100K+ contracts), Perceptyx/Qualtrics/Syndio/KF are enterprise-shaped by DNA. Credit unions have no incumbent at all; higher ed's incumbent is ModernThink (survey-only); gov/K-12 is open field. Precision matters on two fronts: SHL *does* have a motivation questionnaire (attack integration, not existence), and PI is *not* stable (CEO changed March 2026) — keep the cards' exact language.

## 5. Top-3 universal landmines (work against all eight)

1. **"After the results land, who does the work?"** — "When the survey closes, the assessment scores, or the audit finishes — who sits with your managers and builds what happens next, and what does that cost extra?" *(Kills: dashboard-only Experience vendors, SHL's interpretation dependency, KF's post-engagement vacuum, Syndio's remediation treadmill.)*
2. **"Which parts arrived by acquisition, and are they on one data model?"** — "Of the modules in this platform, which were built in-house, which were acquired in the last 24 months, and can the vendor show one combined technical manual and data model?" *(Kills: Phenom, Perceptyx, Qualtrics+PG, KF's Talent Suite — and it's a pure fact question no rep can spin.)*
3. **"Can you measure whether they'll still be here in year two?"** — "Your current tools tell you how someone behaves and how people feel. What tells you whether the person wants this work — and whether they'll still be growing here in two years?" *(Opens the Want×Can frame against every behavior-only, sentiment-only, or matching-only rival on the board.)*
